SCARY TERRY PRODUCTIONS

10 Original Suno Style Boxes — Memphis Underground × Houston Villain Rap

These 10 new hybrids combine the primitive, hypnotic menace of late-1990s Memphis underground rap with the sinister strings, deep bass, and luxurious nocturnal swagger of Houston villain rap.

Each variation explores a different combination of keyboard motifs, string arrangements, drum programming, bass behavior, environmental effects, and production character.

Shared production DNA:

* Drums: Heavy kicks, dry snares, sharp claps, brittle hi-hats, and deliberate negative space.
* Melodies: Primitive two-tone keyboard loops contrasted against detuned chamber strings and glass-like plucks.
* Bass: Deep, controlled mono sub-bass; minimal pitch movement.
* Vocals: Articulate mid-low male delivery, slightly nasal, unhurried, behind the beat, with pronounced punchline pauses.
* Atmosphere: Menacing, mischievous, hypnotic, nocturnal, and unmistakably gangsta.
* Mixing: Memphis cassette grit blended with Houston’s deeper, more luxurious low-end presentation.

The BPM values distinguish the actual tempo grid from the slower perceived half-time groove, consistent with your production glossary. Production-Glossary.pdf

Every style box is under 1,000 characters, and every exclusion list is under 500 characters.

1. VIOLET PANIC PAGER

Character: Cheap two-note pager melody meets luxury-car menace. The most direct marriage of the original concepts.

Weirdness: 39% | Style Likeness: 90%

STYLE BOX — 908 Characters

LATE-1990s MEMPHIS CASSETTE RAP x NOCTURNAL HOUSTON VILLAIN RAP. 170 BPM, 85 BPM half-time lurch. Loop a cheap two-note pager chirp: high-short, low-long, repeated relentlessly. Under it, one bowed cello note hangs motionless; a plucked glass note answers only at the end of four bars. Hard dry kick, papery snare-clap, tiny brittle hats, and rare offbeat rim clicks leave holes. A rounded mono sub hits the roots in short, disciplined pulses, never wandering into modern bass slides. Add one ice clink and a dull car-door thump as punctuation. Drop the chirp for two beats before punchlines, then restart it unchanged. Articulate mid-low male rap sits lazily behind the drums: dry, mildly nasal, smug, unhurried, every consonant clear. Keep the drum machine dusty and nearly mono while the cello spreads slightly wider; cassette hiss and controlled low-end weight make this feel cheap and luxurious at once.

EXCLUSIONS — 324 Characters

mumble rap, speed rap, fast delivery, static, screaming, whispering, poor diction, slurred consonants, autotune singing, literal emergency alarms, police sirens, orchestral swells, busy melodies, bright synth leads, drill 808 glides, phonk cowbells, constant hat rolls, heavy reverb, glossy pop vocals, muddy bass, EDM drops

2. GLASS ELEVATOR STAKEOUT

Character: Sinister penthouse music with primitive Memphis keyboard chirps, expensive strings, and coldly calculated delivery.

Weirdness: 46% | Style Likeness: 88%

STYLE BOX — 922 Characters

MIDNIGHT HOUSTON LUXURY RAP crossed with PRIMITIVE MEMPHIS UNDERGROUND. 144 BPM, 72 BPM half-time cruise. A pinched two-key warning motif ticks every other beat against distant, detuned chamber strings. The melody stays primitive: one high glass pluck, one low keyboard chirp, and a crooked two-note violin reply without becoming a melody. Deep dry kick and brutal center clap anchor a patient, slightly swung groove; closed hats are thin and regular, with rare side-stick and reverse-snare tug. Hold one clean sub pitch for two bars; let kick and bass take turns rather than smearing together. A faint elevator motor, cufflink clink, and car-door latch appear once, not continuously. Male vocal is close, mid-low, lightly nasal, cold and perfectly intelligible, using drawled setups, late entrances, and long punchline silences. Wide strings frame cassette-worn drums; polished bass depth meets grubby Memphis repetition.

EXCLUSIONS — 323 Characters

mumble rap, speed rap, fast delivery, static, screaming, whispering, slurred bars, shouted hooks, heavy autotune, R&B crooning, lush string melodies, bright pop keyboards, constant violin runs, bass slides, busy percussion, trap triplet floods, stadium reverb, EDM builds, cinematic impacts, muddy sub, oversaturated master

3. HYDRAULIC WARNING LIGHT

Character: Mechanical, crooked, and threatening. Faulty dashboard alarms, hydraulic suspension noises, and sinister lowrider swagger.

Weirdness: 51% | Style Likeness: 87%

STYLE BOX — 880 Characters

LO-FI MEMPHIS MENACE with SLOW HOUSTON LOWRIDER PRESSURE. 156 BPM, 78 BPM half-time strut. Use a blunt alternating keyboard beep as the lead, detuned and crooked, like a faulty dashboard. A low cello pedal and two clipped pizzicato-string jabs dress up the bare loop. Give the kick a heavy short attack, layer a brittle handclap over a dry snare, scatter small rim clicks, and keep hats sparse with loose swing. A clean mono bass throb follows the kick; one downward note at the end of a hook is the only flourish. Insert hydraulic suspension creaks, a window switch tick, and one filtered tape-stop on section changes. The rap is mid-low, cool, distinct, nasal-edged and behind the beat, with short emphatic phrases separated by deliberately rude silence. Close dusty drums face off with lightly widened strings; no grand build, just a looping machine that feels rich and broken.

EXCLUSIONS — 331 Characters

mumble rap, speed rap, fast delivery, static, screaming, whispering, hoarse yelling, distorted words, hard autotune, full orchestra, heroic brass, soaring violins, cinematic risers, complex chord progressions, drill bass runs, frequent 808 bends, open-hat washes, trap hat machine-gunning, crash cymbals, EDM drops, glossy club mix

4. PENTHOUSE BOOTLEG

Character: The contrast between expensive Houston production and cheap Memphis cassette instrumentation is the central attraction.

Weirdness: 41% | Style Likeness: 91%

STYLE BOX — 889 Characters

1998 MEMPHIS TAPE-DECK RAP fused with 2 A.M. HOUSTON PENTHOUSE CRUISE. 168 BPM / 84 BPM half-time. Split the beat in two: a stupidly memorable, thin two-tone organ beep in the center, and a velvet-dark, detuned string chord hovering at the edges. Repeat the keys for sixteen bars without resolution; answer it with a single crystalline pluck every fourth bar. Dry punchy kick, cracked snare, clipped clap, tight low-volume hats, and the occasional staggered rimshot create a slow swagger despite the grid. Long, tuned sub notes sustain under the string chord, sidechained subtly around kick strikes, with no flashy slides. Ice taps and faint garage air add place, not clutter. One composed mid-low male rapper delivers clean, nasal-tinged syllables, behind the beat; hooks are flat, repetitive, almost mocking. Warm cassette saturation on drums; clean mono sub and restrained wide strings.

EXCLUSIONS — 337 Characters

mumble rap, speed rap, fast delivery, static, screaming, whispering, mushy consonants, autotuned hooks, pop melodies, triumphant strings, layered vocal choirs, lush pads, constant Foley, thick reverb, multi-part chord changes, booming distorted 808s, 808 pitch slides, huge stereo drums, drill grooves, festival EDM, shiny vocal stacking

5. EXIT-SIGN STRING THEORY

Character: The creepiest variation so far. Repetitive emergency-exit chirps, uncomfortable violin intervals, and unsettling empty space.

Weirdness: 55% | Style Likeness: 85%

STYLE BOX — 884 Characters

HAUNTED MEMPHIS KEYBOARD LOOPS x ICY HOUSTON CHAMBER TRAP. 152 BPM, 76 BPM half-time sway. A high-low emergency-exit chirp plays in a rigid two-beat cycle; the second note drags late for crooked swagger. A lone muted violin scratches one descending minor interval beneath the keys, backed by a faint cello drone and rare glass chimes. Hard muted kick, sharply centered clap, chalk-dry snare, skeletal hats, and side-stick ghosts dominate the open mix. Deep bass holds a single note until the phrase ends; an occasional pitch-down tail marks a reset, never a modern drill glide. Drop strings for entire verses; restore them after drum breaks. Spare fluorescent hum and a single elevator bell hint at upscale danger. Male rap stays clean, mid-low, faintly pinched, menacing through laziness; punchlines land after dead-air pauses. Grainy cassette tone, clean low end, cold stereo edges.

EXCLUSIONS — 322 Characters

mumble rap, speed rap, fast delivery, static, screaming, whispering, shrieking, mumbled doubles, high-pitched rap leads, orchestral horror cues, long melodic violin solos, horror screams, actual fire alarms, bright arpeggios, 808 glissandos, hyperactive hats, huge pad beds, EDM risers, wet vocals, wall-of-sound mastering

6. CRYSTAL CLAP BLACKTOP

Character: The bounciest and most mischievous variation. Crude keyboard notes, unusually punchy claps, and minimal glass-and-string ornamentation.

Weirdness: 37% | Style Likeness: 92%

STYLE BOX — 884 Characters

CLASSIC MEMPHIS UNDERGROUND x CHOPPED HOUSTON NIGHT-RIDE RAP, 160 BPM / 80 BPM half-time. Build a mischievous street-bounce around two squeaky electric-piano notes and a tiny glass strike; repeat it without proper melody. A low pizzicato cello doubles the first note, while faint violin hangs at each fourth-bar end. The drums are tactile and ugly: thumping short kick, loud cracked clap, sandpaper snare, dry rim taps, and restrained swung hat ticks. Place clean mono sub between kicks; make the pattern pulse, not slide. Ice rattles, seatbelt clicks, and a single shuttered-window thud become one-shot percussion. Strip to just kick, clap, and voice for the final word of select bars, then restore the chirp. Articulate male rap is conversational, low-mid, lazily syncopated, and slyly nasal, with crisp stops and deadpan repeated hooks. Grimy mono drums meet narrow luxury strings.

EXCLUSIONS — 335 Characters

mumble rap, speed rap, fast delivery, static, screaming, whispering, growling rap vocals, pitch-corrected crooning, glassy pop lead synths, elaborate chord changes, lush orchestral beds, busy trap percussion, long hat rolls, drill slides, distorted sub, audible crowd chants, dance-pop hooks, big breakdowns, festival drops, vocal wash

7. FREEWAY TELEMETRY

Character: The most Houston-dominant variation. Deep nocturnal cruising energy, sinister strings, and a rudimentary three-hit keyboard pattern.

Weirdness: 49% | Style Likeness: 89%

STYLE BOX — 852 Characters

HOUSTON VILLAIN CRUISE with 1990s MEMPHIS MACHINE-GRIT. 142 BPM, 71 BPM half-time; slow tire-roll swing. Two keys play a dry three-hit pattern: low chirp, high chirp, high chirp, then silence. Detuned viola sits beneath it, one suspicious idea. A subby kick and flinty clap carry the backbeat; hats are tiny and intentionally underplayed, punctuated by rare wooden rims and a backward snare flick. Hold mono sub two bars, then cut it before the next kick. Distant tires on concrete, a toll-booth button, and an ice clink appear as isolated rhythm accents; no continuous street ambience. Male midrange rap is chilly, dry, precisely enunciated, slightly nasal and behind the beat, with cocky pauses and short chant hooks. Surround grimy narrow drums with restrained wide strings and a clean, heavy low end, like luxury speakers playing a filthy cassette.

EXCLUSIONS — 335 Characters

mumble rap, speed rap, fast delivery, static, screaming, whispering, shouted hooks, gravelly vocals, melodic belting, constant road noise, literal alert sirens, phonk cowbells, lush string scores, fast violin ostinatos, repetitive cymbal rolls, modern drill glides, EDM bass, muddy kicks, wide stereo sub, lush reverb, busy transitions

8. VACANT VALET LOOP

Character: Closest to the original Memphis alarm concept, but injected with Houston’s controlled sub-bass and dark chamber-string sophistication.

Weirdness: 44% | Style Likeness: 90%

STYLE BOX — 854 Characters

MINIMAL MEMPHIS ALARM RAP in a SHADOWY HOUSTON VALET GARAGE. 172 BPM counted double, heard as an 86 BPM half-time head-nod. A tacky two-tone organ honks: one clipped note, one quarter-beat ring. Distant cello harmonics and one crystal pluck mark turnarounds. A rattly drum-machine kick, bone-dry clap/snare, light rimshot, and narrow hats form a loose, patient pocket; leave entire beats exposed. A punchy mono bass note shadows the kick, with one slow sub fall per chorus, never running slides. Spare valet-ticket rip, car-key rattle, and garage-gate click replace flashy transition effects. Male mid-low rap arrives behind the beat, nasal-edged, sharply intelligible, with swaggering sentence fragments and deliberately held silence. Keep the lead and drums small, tape-grained, and mostly centered while understated strings add depth without swelling.

9. CROOKED CHANDELIER

Character: An unusually dark hybrid. The keyboard loop sounds almost comical until the cello turns everything sinister.

Weirdness: 58% | Style Likeness: 86%

STYLE BOX — 865 Characters

MEMPHIS TAPE RAP meets GOTHIC HOUSTON LUXURY LOWRIDER. 148 BPM / 74 BPM half-time, patient and crooked. A primitive two-note call-and-response runs between a thin keyboard chirp and a high glass-mallet click; playful notes turn grim against a detuned cello semitone. Hold a minor string interval, not a chord sequence. The drum pocket has a deep dry kick, hard clap slightly late, splintery snare ghost, and barely-there hats, with deliberate one-bar dropouts. A pure mono sub sustains low underneath, briefly ducked by the kick; no melody in the bass. Sprinkle in chandelier-glass taps, a faint mechanical hum, and one reverse-snare drag every eight bars. The rapper sounds mid-low, close-miked, male, lucid, dry, mildly nasal and lazily behind the beat, stopping punchlines early and leaving silence. Center cassette grit against understated wide chamber strings.

EXCLUSIONS — 330 Characters

mumble rap, speed rap, fast delivery, static, screaming, whispering, harsh yelling, sentimental singing, hard autotune, busy chord progressions, classical orchestral crescendos, horror-movie jump scares, major-key synths, lush pads, bass glides, drill hat rolls, thick distortion, high-gloss EDM, massive reverb, giant snare tails

10. VELVET MALFUNCTION

Character: The most balanced blend of the two originals, emphasizing threatening repetition, controlled sub pressure, sinister strings, and occasional mechanical weirdness.

Weirdness: 53% | Style Likeness: 88%

STYLE BOX — 885 Characters

UNDERGROUND MEMPHIS CASSETTE RHYTHM grafted onto ICY HOUSTON PENTHOUSE RAP. 164 BPM, 82 BPM half-time. A crooked two-tone car-sensor beep repeats over low violin drone. Every four bars, glass harmonics answer; a cello pluck replaces the last kick. Drums are sparse and commanding: thick dry kick, severe clap, brittle snare edge, little swung hats, and a single ghost rim just before occasional downbeats. The bass is deep, clean, centered, and nearly fixed, with one pitch fall at section end. Add isolated glass-on-marble ticks, a remote door lock click, and occasional reverse-snare suction, never a busy FX montage. Male rapper: mid-low register, sharply articulated, faint nasal bite, slouched behind-the-beat confidence; deadpan refrains and long air gaps make every word land. Unpolished tape texture and almost-mono drums contrast with polished sub and restrained wide strings.

EXCLUSIONS — 332 Characters

mumble rap, speed rap, fast delivery, static, screaming, whispering, slurry consonants, raspy shouting, melodic autotune, melodic pop choruses, big string ensembles, too many chord changes, actual sirens, orchestral impacts, heavy tape hiss, muddy mix, phonk cowbells, trap hat rolls, drill 808 slides, EDM drops, blown-out clipping

Recommended Starting Points

#1 — Violet Panic Pager: The strongest direct synthesis of both original references. It preserves the stupidly catchy two-tone Memphis loop while incorporating Houston strings, bass weight, and calculated villain delivery.

#4 — Penthouse Bootleg: Best for the contrast between crude cassette-era instrumentation and expensive nocturnal production. Particularly suitable for repetitive hooks and deliberate punchline spacing.

#6 — Crystal Clap Blacktop: Best for mischievous, cocky rap. Its punchier rhythm and stripped-down melodic design should leave plenty of room for dense wordplay without encouraging rushed delivery.

#9 — Crooked Chandelier: Most unconventional harmonic character. The intentionally uncomfortable relationship between playful keyboard notes and sinister cello intervals could produce the most distinctive instrumental.

#10 — Velvet Malfunction: Best overall balance of hypnotic repetition, sinister strings, deep bass, and unusual production details.

My first three Suno tests would be #1, #6, and #10, using the same lyrics for all three to compare how their different grooves handle the vocal cadence.
