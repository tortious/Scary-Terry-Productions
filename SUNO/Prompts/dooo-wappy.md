# SUNO V6 — SMOKE, SWAGGER & BAD INTENTIONS

**15 snazzy, smoky, upbeat, darkly comedic crooner-rap productions**

## The Concept

A slick, wisecracking lounge singer with the vocabulary of a gangster, the confidence of a mob boss, and absolutely no moral compass.

Picture a dimly lit 1950s supper club, a crooked Vegas casino, or a smoky jazz lounge—except the singer alternates between smooth baritone crooning, rhythmic talk-singing, and hard, articulate rap verses.

The music should be genuinely catchy, mischievous, sophisticated, and upbeat, with darkly comedic undertones.

## Musical Palette

Musically, we're combining:

- **1950s–1960s crooner jazz:** Swinging piano, upright bass, brushed drums, muted brass.
- **Vintage lounge and nightclub music:** Hammond organ, vibraphone, finger snaps, walking basslines.
- **Boom-bap and Southern hip-hop:** Punchy snares, substantial low end, head-nodding grooves.
- **Vaudeville, doo-wop, and cabaret:** Theatrical arrangements, call-and-response, memorable hooks.
- **Dark comedy:** Playful, slightly crooked melodies accompanying outrageous, arrogant lyrics.

## Important Vocal Distinction

Unlike your usual strictly rapped productions, these intentionally feature **both rapping and singing**.

The ideal performance is:

> Deep masculine baritone → conversational rhythmic rap → playful melodic talk-singing → silky crooned hook → back to dry, articulate rap.

No delicate pop vocals, excessive falsetto, theatrical screaming, or frantic speed-rapping.

Every variation includes a complete Suno V6 Style Box, Exclusions, BPM, key, Weirdness, Style Influence, and vocal-direction cues.

## BONUS — GETTING THE RAP/CROON BALANCE RIGHT

One potential problem with these hybrid styles is that Suno may interpret *crooner* as permission to sing the entire song.

For your material, I'd aim for:

| Vocal Approach | Approximate Share | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Rhythmic rap | 50% | Verses, intricate rhymes, punchlines |
| Melodic crooning | 30% | Catchy hooks and memorable refrains |
| Rhythmic talk-singing | 20% | Transitions, bridges, sarcastic accents |

> These are creative targets, not settings that Suno can enforce precisely.

### Universal Vocal Style Add-On

Add this to any Style Box that needs more deliberate switching between rapping and singing:

> **VOCAL ARRANGEMENT:** Deep masculine baritone with three distinct modes: precise, rhythmically spoken rap verses; playful melodic talk-singing during transitions; and smooth vintage crooning on hooks. Alternate naturally between techniques. Sung sections must have clear memorable melodies, while rap sections retain a strong hip-hop pocket, crisp diction and deadpan comedic attitude.

### Universal Exclusions

speed rap, double-time rap, rapid-fire flow, mumble rap, marble-mouth diction, whispering, screaming, shouting, distorted lead vocals, slurred pronunciation, muddy vocal mixing, excessive autotune, weak percussion, overly sentimental ballads, high-pitched pop vocals, excessive falsetto, operatic singing, frantic scat singing, excessive vocal harmonies, chaotic brass solos, overcrowded instrumentation

## Best Directions for Your Lyrics

- **For unapologetically vulgar, arrogant, darkly comedic material:** Start with **#15 — The Unrepentant Showman**. Its upbeat doo-wop, hard boom-bap drums, and charismatic crooning provide maximum contrast.
- **For something sophisticated and genuinely musically impressive:** **#01 — The Devil Wears a Tuxedo** combines classic lounge swing with punchy hip-hop and a versatile low-register vocal.
- **For the strangest, most distinctive result:** **#14 — The Grim Reaper's Lounge Act** introduces eerie glass harmonics and Memphis-style bass without sacrificing the snazzy nightclub feel.
- **For a smoother, more replayable sound:** **#10 — The Velvet Villain** emphasizes Rhodes, vibraphone, G-funk bass movement, relaxed rap, and memorable crooned hooks.

## Recommended Suno V6 Settings

- For the more conventional swing and lounge hybrids, start with **40–55% Weirdness** and **88–93% Style Influence**.
