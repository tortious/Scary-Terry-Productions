Below are 10 variations of your original late-1990s Memphis underground rap style box, designed for Suno v6.

All 10 preserve the same fundamental sound: primitive Memphis production, hypnotic repetition, eerie but playful melodies, hard skeletal drums, enormous negative space, and dry, deadpan, clearly articulated male rap.

I’ve deliberately kept the variations subtle rather than turning them into different subgenres. The primary differences involve keyboard textures, percussion accents, melodic motifs, and minor atmospheric adjustments.

Every style box is under 1,000 characters, and every corresponding exclusion list is under 500 characters.

1. BASEMENT CASIO MENACE

Character: Closest to your original. Primitive, catchy, minimal, and slightly unsettling.

Weirdness: 24% | Style Likeness: 88%

STYLE BOX — 906 Characters

Late-1990s Memphis underground rap, 171 BPM with an 85–86 BPM half-time pocket. Crude, eerie, playful, repetitive, and almost childishly simple. Center everything on a thin, slightly detuned Casio-style keyboard playing a two-note minor-key figure. Add a blunt dry kick, sharp drum-machine snare, faint clap doubling, ticking straight hats, and an occasional rim click. Short sub-bass notes shadow the kick; no melodic bass movement or slides. Keep long stretches of empty space and recycle the same hypnotic loop, changing only a kick placement, hat mute, or one-bar dropout. Male rap vocal: dry, clearly enunciated, deadpan, nasal-to-midrange, and calmly behind the beat. Short taunting phrases should recur like a sinister schoolyard chant. Hooks stay plain and sticky, not sung. Narrow near-mono image, dusty digital grain, light cassette hiss, barely any reverb, and a raw Southern basement-tape feel.

My top recommendations for your Scary Terry material

#6 — VHS Keyboard Stab: Best overall. Its relaxed, deliberately simple vocal direction should complement longer, densely written verses without encouraging Suno to rush through them. The electric-piano motif also offers a good balance of menace and catchiness.

#1 — Basement Casio Menace: Best for authenticity. This stays closest to the source prompt and should be a useful baseline for comparing the other nine.

#3 — Toy Piano Terror: Best for dark humor. The absurdly simple keyboard motif could create an effective contrast against ridiculous or aggressively menacing punchlines.

#7 — Square-Wave Shakedown: Best for harder drums. The additional percussive movement may give the lyrics more physical impact while retaining the primitive Memphis aesthetic.

#9 — Backroom Mallet Loop: Best for lyrical intelligibility. The restrained instrumentation and relaxed delivery instructions leave substantial room for your words to remain prominent.

A note about the Suno settings

The percentages are suggested starting points, not guarantees of how Suno will interpret each prompt.

For your particular preference for clear diction, controlled pacing, minimal accompaniment, and an ice-cold deadpan delivery, I’d generally favor:

* Weirdness: 24–32%
* Style Likeness: 85–90%

I’d avoid pushing Weirdness much higher initially, because the unusual keyboard textures and primitive production already provide plenty of character. The higher values may also lead to less predictable vocal phrasing.

Suggested testing order: #6 → #1 → #3 → #9 → #7. This gives you five subtly different instrumental approaches while keeping the core vocal requirements and Memphis atmosphere consistent.
