## Dance With the Devil

### Style Box

Underground East Coast narrative hip-hop, 89 BPM, minor-key noir, long-form story rap. Build around a stark repeating piano figure with cold low strings, faint choir-like shadow tones, restrained sub-bass, dry boom-bap kick, hard centered snare, sparse closed hats and almost no ornamentation. Keep the loop hypnotic and increasingly oppressive rather than evolving into a cinematic climax. Male lead: low, serious, fully articulated, controlled chest voice; conversational cadence that locks tightly to the pocket, then subtly intensifies as the story darkens. Favor complete sentences, internal rhymes, deliberate pauses and vivid scene-setting over punchline density. Chorus presence should be minimal and severe, with little melodic lift. Mix vocals close, dry and dominant; narrow vintage stereo image, dusty sample texture, light tape saturation, restrained mastering. Overall feeling: urban tragedy, moral dread, fatalism, claustrophobia, cold realism, escalating psychological tension.

### Exclusions

whisper vocals, mumble rap, screaming, speed rap, rapid double-time, melodic trap, autotune crooning, sung pop chorus, drill percussion, sliding 808s, rage synths, phonk cowbells, EDM drops, glossy modern pop mix, bright piano, triumphant strings, lush ambient pads, cheerful harmony, trap hi-hat rolls, busy percussion, rock guitars, pop-punk drums, soulful vocal runs, playful ad-libs, comedy, party energy, heroic trailer drums, huge reverbs, airy vocals, uplifting endings

## Forgot About Dre

### Style Box

Turn-of-the-millennium West Coast studio rap, 134 BPM with a strong 67 BPM half-time pocket, dark polished menace, precise stop-start arrangement. Use a clipped minor-key string motif, muted guitar-like stabs, taut electric bass, punchy kick, razor-dry snare, tight closed hats and sparse percussion accents. Leave conspicuous pockets of silence around vocal phrases; keep every hit clean and intentional. Structure for two contrasting male rap voices: first voice deep, calm, authoritative and behind the beat; second voice brighter, sharper, more animated, rhythmically dense and sharply articulated. Alternate measured swagger with bursts of compact multisyllabic phrasing without turning into speed rap. Chorus should hit as a forceful chant, not sung melody. Mix vocals extremely forward and dry, drums hard but uncluttered, bass firm rather than sub-heavy, stereo image disciplined. Overall vibe: expensive, confrontational, clinical, muscular, sarcastic, controlled, intimidating.

### Exclusions

whisper vocals, mumble rap, screaming, breathless speed rap, sustained double-time, melodic trap, autotune crooning, emo delivery, drill slides, rage synths, phonk cowbells, EDM drops, lo-fi haze, dusty jazz loops, lush soul samples, dreamy pads, soft snares, washed reverb, trap hi-hat spam, sing-song hooks, loose timing, slurred diction, sentimental tone, cheerful melodies, pop choruses, ambient drift, huge trailer drums, chaotic percussion, booming modern sub-bass, glossy dance-pop sheen

## Hood Shit

### Style Box

Mid-2000s Chicago street rap, slow heavy half-time feel, stripped block-music production, blunt menace. Center the beat on a huge compact kick, dry cracking snare, sparse ticking hats, low sustained bass and one dark minor-key keyboard or synth phrase that repeats almost unchanged. Add occasional eerie bell or string accents; keep harmonic movement minimal so the rapper supplies the momentum. Male lead should sound deep, heavy, unhurried and fully intelligible, with short forceful bars, spaces between phrases, hard line-ending emphasis and a conversational street cadence sitting slightly behind the beat. Hook should be primitive, repetitive and chant-like, built for instant recall rather than melody. Keep ad-libs sparse and low. Mix close, dry, bass-forward and somewhat narrow, with mixtape-era grit. Overall vibe: nighttime city blocks, intimidation, street authority, raw local realism, low-slung swagger, blunt repetition, pressure without theatricality.

### Exclusions

whisper vocals, mumble rap, screaming, speed rap, nonstop double-time, autotune singing, melodic trap, emo rap, glossy pop hooks, modern drill slides, rage synths, phonk cowbells, EDM drops, bright synth leads, lush pads, orchestral swells, cinematic trailer drums, acoustic guitars, pop-punk, busy percussion, rapid trap hats, airy reverb, dreamy atmosphere, cheerful melody, sentimental vocals, polished radio sheen, complex chord changes, dance-pop bounce, playful ad-libs, soft vocal delivery

## Falling Down

### Style Box

Late-2010s emo rap and alternative trap, 120 BPM with a spacious 60 BPM half-time feel, melancholic guitar-centered harmony, intimate bedroom atmosphere. Use a washed electric-guitar or processed plucked motif, soft sustained pads, warm rounded sub-bass, muted kick, restrained snare or clap, sparse hats and gentle rolls. Let guitar ambience and vocal tails fill negative space instead of dense percussion. Two male voices trade compact melodic sections: fragile sing-rap phrasing, clear but weary diction, elongated vowels, restrained pitch correction, soft doubles and distant harmonies. Verses feel conversational and vulnerable; hooks should be simple, repetitive, immediate and emotionally heavy. Keep transitions smooth and understated. Mix wide and hazy around the instruments but keep the lead intimate and readable. Overall vibe: rain-soaked night, grief, numbness, longing, faded memories, youthful isolation, bittersweet beauty, raw confession.

### Exclusions

screaming, aggressive shouting, speed rap, technical boom-bap flow, hard drill cadence, phonk cowbells, rage synths, EDM drops, festival bass, gritty boom-bap drums, dry mono vocals, cinematic trailer drums, jazz samples, funk bass, bright dance-pop chords, country guitar, metal riffs, harsh distortion, dense percussion, relentless trap rolls, triumphant brass, comedy, battle-rap aggression, gangsta delivery, raw untreated vocals, upbeat party energy, cheerful hooks, rigid machine-gun cadence

## Money in a Ziplock Bag

### Style Box

Early-2000s underground street rap with a regional West Coast-leaning groove, murky minor-key loop, hustler atmosphere and rough CD-R mixtape texture. Build the beat from a dark keyboard figure, faint G-funk-style synth coloration, thick rounded bass, blunt kick, dry snare, simple hats and sparse percussion. Keep the loop repetitive, funky and slightly sinister. Use two contrasting male rap approaches: one grounded, conversational and streetwise with a steady mid-tempo pocket; the other more elastic, nasal and rhythmically agile, shifting between quick syllabic flurries and melodic inflections while remaining intelligible. Hook should feel chantable and raw, with sparse doubles rather than a polished sung chorus. Keep reverb short, vocals close, low end dominant, stereo width modest and mastering rugged. Overall vibe: cash counting, parking-lot night air, cheap studio smoke, street business, nervous energy, regional swagger, grimy bounce, unpolished character.

### Exclusions

whisper vocals, mumble rap, screaming, constant speed rap, nonstop double-time, glossy autotune singing, melodic trap, modern drill slides, rage synths, phonk cowbells, EDM drops, hyperpop, bright pop chords, polished radio mastering, lush ambient pads, sentimental hooks, acoustic ballad guitar, pop-punk, trailer drums, giant sub drops, clean digital sheen, busy trap hats, cheerful melody, smooth R&B chorus, airy reverb, soft delivery, festival energy, futuristic sound design

## The 10 Crack Commandments

### Style Box

Mid-1990s East Coast boom-bap, 87 BPM, dark sample-driven minimalism, disciplined head-nod groove, instructional rap. Build around one eerie chopped piano, bell or horn-like sample phrase repeating with minimal harmonic development; pair it with thick rounded bass, heavy compressed kick, dry cracking snare, crisp closed hats, sparse percussion and faint vinyl grit. Male lead should be deep, calm, authoritative and intelligible, delivered like street rules explained face-to-face. Use a deliberate mid-tempo cadence, short declarative bars, hard end-rhyme placement, internal rhymes, strategic pauses and conversational swing. Keep ad-libs minimal. Chorus should function as a stark refrain or reset, not a melodic pop hook. Mix vocals centered, close and dry; drums punchy and compact; stereo image narrow, sample dusty, mastering forceful, not glossy. Overall vibe: basement cypher, coded wisdom, cold realism, measured confidence, hypnotic repetition, grim wit, methodical street narration.

### Exclusions
